Gap to target

What the designs require that the backend cannot yet hold.

The screens in this project define the end state of the product. The backend was built ahead of them, from requirements derived while the concept was still moving — so most of what differs is not a defect, it is a place where the target only recently became specific. This document names those places, flow by flow, read against loveiqhq/showup main @ c195089. Seven items still need a decision; two were decided on 6 August and are now build specs. The rest already agrees.

How to read this

Three things worth stating before the list, because they change what the list means.

i

The designs are the target; the code is provisional

Nothing below is phrased as a bug. Where the two disagree, the design wins by definition — the question is only when the code should follow, and whether the design implies a data model nobody has agreed to yet.

ii

Tracking is already reconciled — this is about the product data model

Event Taxonomy v1.2 was reconciled against this exact commit, and its twenty conflicts are closed. This report deliberately does not re-cover that ground. It covers a different surface: whether the backend can store and serve what the screens collect and display.

iii

The repo is backend only

loveiqhq/showup is a NestJS + PostGIS API. There is no frontend in it, so there is nothing to compare visually — no components, no CSS, no token file. Colour, type and layout adherence cannot drift yet because no client consumes the design system. That comparison becomes possible when the iOS/Android clients land, and is worth re-running then.

2

The nine gaps

7 open · 2 decided

Ordered by how much rework each causes if it is decided late. Each carries the owner who has to make the call — Product for a decision about the experience, Engineering for one about implementation.

01

Six of nine profile steps have nowhere to live EngineeringProduct

The single largest gap, and the one that blocks the most screens. The profile entity carries nine columns; the designed flow collects nine fields, and only two of them (name, DOB) plus free-text bio have a home.

Designedheight · gender · orientation · habits · interests (max 10) · prompts (pairs) · living situation · age · plus a visibility flag per field
In codedisplayName · dateOfBirth · bio · gender varchar(40) · lookingFor · isVisible · isComplete · verificationStatus · moderationStanding

Decision needed: two separate calls. Shape — typed columns, one JSONB attributes blob, or child tables; interests and prompts are multi-row and capped, so they want tables regardless of what the scalars do. Vocabulary ownership — the option sets already exist, agreed, on Enums §1. Those should become Postgres enums generated from that registry rather than re-typed by hand, or the two will diverge on the first edit.

Why it is urgent: matching and discovery read profile attributes. Choosing the shape late means rewriting the query layer, not just adding a migration.

02

"Don't display on my profile" has no persisted state Engineering

The checkbox appears on all nine profile-creation steps, unticked by default. The taxonomy resolved its semantics (conflict 07c): display is suppressed, matching is unaffected, and the opt-out is tracked as one directional event with no boolean.

That resolution explicitly requires the completion-time state to be authoritative over the event stream — because a user who ticks and then unticks must end up displayed. There is nowhere to keep that state. Today the answer is only inferable from analytics, which is exactly what the decision ruled out.

Decision needed: a per-field visibility store — a profile_field_visibility row per hidden field, or a mask on the profile. Whichever shape gap 01 takes, this rides along with it, so decide the two together.

03

Discovery has no filters, and lookingFor contradicts the taxonomy Engineering

Matching centres on the viewer's active check-in and searches a hard-coded DEFAULT_DISCOVERY_RADIUS_METERS = 50_000. There is no filter state anywhere in the service: no gender filter, no orientation filter, no age range, no user-set distance.

Designedmeet_gender multi · meet_orientation multi · age_range 18–99 · distance_km user-set
In codelookingFor varchar(40), single value, on the profile — unread by matching

Decision needed: the taxonomy already resolved that looking_for is search state, not a profile attribute (Enums §10). The column therefore sits in the wrong place. Move it to a discovery-preferences table over the same enums as the profile fields — one vocabulary, no mapping table — and have getDiscovery read it.

04

The 10-minute grace rule does not exist in the date lifecycle ProductEngineering

The Late cancellation screen encodes a specific, symmetrical promise: past ten minutes the person waiting may leave with no penalty, and the cost lands on the person who broke it. The backend has one cancellation path — cancel(dateId, userId, reason?) — which records who cancelled and an optional free-text reason. Nothing models lateness, the grace window, or the asymmetry.

Missing, concretely: a "running late" declaration from the late party; a timestamp the grace window is measured from; a distinct terminal state for date closed because one side left inside the rules as against an ordinary cancellation; and the rule that the waiter's score is untouched.

Decision needed: Product confirms the ten minutes is fixed and universal (the screen states it as a brand promise, so presumably yes). Engineering then adds the states — this is a lifecycle change, not a field.

05

Show-up Rate is displayed everywhere and computed nowhere Product

Screens show a concrete number and a concrete movement — 94% → 90%. The backend stores the inputs (aRating, bRating, aConfirmedHappened, bConfirmedHappened, plus a permanent status-change history per date, which is a good foundation) but derives no score. Scoring is Epic 8, not started.

Decision needed, and it is a Product decision: the formula. What counts as a miss, how much a late cancellation costs, whether the window is lifetime or rolling, what a new user starts at, and whether the number is shown to others or only to its owner. Every screen quoting a percentage is unbuildable until this is answered — and a placeholder formula will be visible to users the moment it is wrong, so it should not be guessed.

06

The 270-event taxonomy has no ingestion point Not started

modules/analytics/analytics.module.ts is a 175-byte placeholder. PostHog is configured at boot (config/configuration.ts) and the taxonomy is complete and reconciled, but no event in it is emitted or received yet. Roughly a third are marked Server, which means they cannot be added by the client later — they need trigger points in the services the taxonomy already names.

Not a conflict — a sequencing note. Worth stating plainly so nobody assumes tracking is live because the spec is finished. The taxonomy's per-event source anchors (auth/token.service.ts and so on) are the build list.

07

OTP length — six digits Decided · 6 digits

Confirmed by Product, 6 Aug 2026: six digits. The design is the truth; the code changes. Consistent with conflict 02. Still four digits in otp.service.ts at this commit, and not yet developed — a two-line change with a test alongside it. Applies to both code paths: the phone SMS OTP and the email OTP in item 08.

08

Email capture and verification Decided · build it

Confirmed by Product, 6 Aug 2026: an email address will be requested during profile creation, and it must be verified by a 6-digit code. Not developed yet; the design is the target. This matches conflict 03, which kept all five email events rather than removing them.

Note the two code screens are different things and neither replaces the other. Verify code is phone verification, flow 01 · Welcome & sign-up — that is sign-in, and auth/otp.service.ts backs it. Verify email is labelled 03 · Profile creation · Email verification, step 2 of 3 of The basics, and runs after the user is authenticated by phone. There is no phone-versus-email identity conflict.

To build, concretely: an email column on the account or profile; an outbound mail sender; a second OTP path distinct from the SMS one, also 6 digits, with its own resend cooldown and attempt counter; and a verification state so a collected-but-unverified address is distinguishable from a verified one. The five Family D events (email_submitted, email_validation_failed, email_code_sent, email_code_resent, email_code_submitted) already carry their full spec and flip from Not built to Live once it exists — no taxonomy change needed.

Still worth settling before the migration: whether a verified email is ever the only way to reach or recover an account. If yes, it is an identity credential and needs the recovery path designed; if it is for notifications only, the column and the verification are enough.

09

Profile completion ignores the detail steps Product

isProfileComplete requires a display name, a date of birth, and four photos. Nine designed steps sit outside it, so a profile passes as complete with every one of them skipped — which makes matching thinner than the designs assume and makes the "complete" flag weaker than the flow implies.

Decision needed: whether any detail step is required, or a minimum count across them, or completion stays photo-led on purpose to keep onboarding short. A legitimate answer is "leave it" — but it should be a decision, since the design of the flow implies otherwise.

4

Keeping it aligned

This report is a snapshot of one commit. Three habits make the next one cheap.

a

One vocabulary, generated not retyped

The option sets on Enums §1 and §10 are agreed. If they are hand-copied into TypeScript enums they will drift on the first edit. Generate the Postgres and TypeScript enums from that registry, or make the registry a file in the repo that both this document and the code read.

b

Re-run this diff at each commit that touches an entity

The expensive gaps here are all shape decisions — where a field lives, what states a lifecycle has. Those are cheap before the migration and expensive after. A re-read when profile, date or match entities change catches them at the cheap end.

c

Decide gaps 01, 02 and 03 as one piece of work

They are the same decision seen three times: where profile attributes and search preferences live, and over which vocabulary. Answered together they are one migration. Answered separately they are three, and the third will contradict the first.
