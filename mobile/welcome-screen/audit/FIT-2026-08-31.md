# Does it fit? Every screen, every state, eighteen phone sizes

31 August 2026 · Android, measured. iOS, not measured — see the last section.

## What was done

Every screen in the welcome/sign-up flow and the tutorial, in every state either can be in — 43
screen states in all — rendered at 18 phone sizes from the 320x568 iPhone SE to the 440x956
iPhone 16 Pro Max, and measured. 774 renders.

Not previews, and not screenshots by eye. The real Compose runtime lays each screen out and the
result is measured: what got clipped, what collapsed, what ended up outside the frame, what tap
target came out too small to hit.

Content is rendered into the **safe area**, not the raw screen. A 390x844 iPhone does not have
844dp to play with — the status bar and the gesture bar take about 81 between them, and it is
precisely the content pushed into those strips that gets lost. Each device carries its own figures.

## The headline

**Every phone 390dp wide or larger is completely clean.** iPhone 12 through 16 Pro Max, all the
Plus and Pro Max sizes, Pixel 4a/5/6/7/8. Nothing clipped, nothing collapsed, no tap target under
size, in any state. The design holds at the sizes it was drawn for.

Everything below is short screens, and every one of them is the same underlying problem: **the
bottom band is given whatever height is left over, and on a short screen there is nothing left.**
The legal line, the help line and sometimes the button above them get squeezed to nothing. They do
not move off the screen — Compose shrinks them in place — so nothing looks obviously broken in a
preview. The text is simply not all there.

## A · Affects 375x667, which the tickets require evidence for

SHOWUP-140/142/143 all name 375 x 667 as a size the screenshots must cover, so these block sign-off.

| Screen | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Welcome back, 24-character name** | "Trouble signing in?…" and "Legal Notice · Privacy Policy" both collapse to **zero height** — they are not on the screen at all |
| **Connect, after a cancel or an error** | "By continuing you agree to our Terms and Privacy Policy." is clipped, and on shorter phones disappears |

The 24-character name is not an edge case someone invented: SHOWUP-142 lists it as a required
case, and the same ticket says *"The four buttons plus the legal line must be fully above the safe
area."* Today, on that phone, the legal line is not there at all.

The Connect case only appears **after something goes wrong** — cancel, no network, declined. The
idle screen is fine, which is why it has not been noticed.

## B · Affects common small Android phones

360x640, 360x740 and 360x780 are ordinary current devices, not museum pieces.

- **Welcome back** — the legal line is clipped for every `lastUsed` value, not only long names.
- **Connect** — the same legal line problem as above, one size worse.

## C · Only the 2016 iPhone SE (320x568)

Lowest priority: this is the smallest screen iOS still runs, and the share is tiny. Listed for
completeness rather than as a call to action.

- **Startup** — "Already have an account? Log in" is crushed to **9.5dp** tall. Effectively untappable.
- **Tutorial card 1** — "Takes less than a minute" disappears.
- **Tutorial card 2** — the "Next" button is 25.5dp.
- **Tutorial card 6** — "I'm ready to show up" and "Back" both disappear. The tour cannot be finished.

## Correction: the phone and code screens were not measured the first time

The first run reported these two screens as clean. They were not clean, they were **absent**: both
call `focusRequester.requestFocus()` when they open, which throws whenever the field is not attached
to a window, and that killed the sweep before it measured anything. The suite reported a failure,
the failure looked like the others, and the gap went unnoticed for a run.

The focus request is now non-fatal -- a convenience that opens the keyboard should never be able to
crash a screen, on a device or anywhere else -- and both screens are measured. Two real findings
came straight out of it, neither of them a squeeze on a small phone:

| Screen | Finding | Devices |
|---|---|---|
| **Phone number** | The input measured to the height of its own text -- **23dp inside a 56dp row** -- and the row centred it. The field LOOKED 56dp tall while only the middle 23dp would accept a tap. | 15 of 18 |
| **Code** | "Edit phone number" came to **27dp**, below any usable minimum, on the compact layouts -- including 375x667. | 5 of 18 |

The first one is the more interesting: it is invisible. Nothing is clipped, nothing overflows,
every screenshot looks perfect, and a person tapping the top of the field just watches nothing
happen. No amount of looking at pictures finds that.

Both are fixed.

## D · Found by CI, not reproducible on the development machine

*1 September 2026. The most interesting finding in this document, for a reason that has nothing to
do with the layout.*

**The phone screen's error messages are clipped on every phone 360dp wide or narrower** — about
20px is cut off, so the reader sees two lines of a three-line sentence. It is not one message but
the whole family: every error that carries an example number, which is five of the seven. Devices:
320x686, 360x640, 360x740, 360x780, 360x800. All current, none exotic.

Each names the country and gives an example number, so they are long, and at 360dp they need a
third line the surrounding column has no room to give. The example is also the most useful part of
the message -- it is what tells someone what a valid number looks like -- so the obvious cut is the
expensive one.

**It does not reproduce on Windows.** Same commit, same test, same 17 devices: green locally, red on
the Linux runner. The two platforms lay out text fractionally differently, and this sentence sits
exactly on the boundary between two lines and three.

That is what makes it a real defect rather than a marginal one. **Whether a user sees the whole
message currently depends on their device's font rendering** — it is not that the layout is tight,
it is that it is on a knife edge, and which side it falls is luck.

**Not fixed here**, because the fix is a choice and there are three:

1. **Shorten the message.** It currently names the country and gives an example. Dropping the
   example would fit, and would cost the most useful part.
2. **Give the region a third line** on compact screens, taking the space from the gap below it.
   Moves the CTA up on small phones, which SHOWUP-143 explicitly forbids.
3. **Let the screen scroll** when it is this tight. The frame already asks for that
   (`scrollWhenTight = true`) and it is evidently not taking effect here — possibly the real bug.

Option 3 is where I would look first: the intent is already expressed in the code and appears not
to work, which is a different and better problem to have than a copy decision.

**Worth stating plainly:** every layout finding in this document was produced on one machine until
now. This one proves that was never enough, and that the value of building on a second platform is
not redundancy but disagreement.

## Fixed in this pass

- **Home placeholder, "Start over"** — came out 43dp, one below the smallest comfortable tap
  target on any phone. Now 48. This screen is scaffolding for SHOWUP-146, so the fix is free.
- **Phone number input** — now fills its row, so the touch target matches the field a person sees.
- **"Edit phone number"** — given a 44dp minimum touch height. It looks identical; only the area
  that answers to a finger changed.

Nothing else was changed. Every remaining item touches a screen in PO Acceptance, and the fix is a
layout decision — give the bottom band a minimum height and let the flexible middle yield first —
which is the designer's call, not one to make silently.

## How much to trust this

The harness is itself tested, by `HarnessSelfTest`, which feeds it cases whose answer is known: it
must catch clipped text, an element outside the frame and an undersized tap target, and it must
stay silent on a clean layout and on a compliant 56dp button. A measuring instrument nobody has
checked measures nothing.

Two false detectors were found and removed while building it, both of which would have produced
confident nonsense:

- Comparing the rightmost line extent against the node width flagged every **centred** headline as
  overflowing. Caught because the reported overflow *grew* with the screen — 24px at 360dp, 54px at
  390dp, 92px at 428dp — which is the opposite of how clipping behaves.
- Holding every tappable thing to the 56dp button minimum flagged the 44dp "Log in" link, the 50dp
  "Use a different account", the 52dp skip row and the 54dp sheet button on every device — about
  1,300 lines of noise burying the real findings. The bar is now 44dp to fail (Apple's stated
  minimum, and the smallest value this design uses deliberately), with 44–56 reported and not
  failed.

## iOS

**Not measured.** There is no Mac in this environment, so the SwiftUI screens cannot be built or
laid out here — this is the same constraint recorded in `CountryCodes.swift` and the 144/145 audit.

The iOS screens are ports of the same designs with the same structure, so the findings above are
very likely to apply there too, and the Connect and Welcome back cases almost certainly do. That
is an inference, not a measurement, and it should not be written down as a result. Confirming it
needs one run of the equivalent harness on a Mac, or SwiftUI previews at the sizes in section A.
